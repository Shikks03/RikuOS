A — fix it now.

On a narrow phone screen, the left strip turns into a thin bar across the top instead, and the page gets the phone’s full width. Both the “add an event” and “add a to-do” buttons work at this width. The cost: the small coloured dots that show whether each background helper is running currently have no place to sit on a phone, so they disappear from the phone menu until a proper phone design happens.

B — leave it.

The page stays built for a laptop screen for now; a full phone design comes later, on its own. The cost: with the menu strip still eating a third of the screen, the page is too narrow to open either the “add an event” or “add a to-do” box at all — both buttons are switched off. The whole reason this page exists — the phone buzzes at 7am, you open it, you deal with your day — doesn’t really work yet on a phone.

170px fixed rail 164px page at menu strip · 334px page at top bar form floor ≈213px of tile width 17 characters before a to-do title truncates at 164px

What this changes: this is the menu for the whole app, on every phone-width screen — not just this page. A fixes it now as a placeholder until a real phone-design round. B changes nothing now, and a phone user is limited to reading the morning notification until that later round.

The lead recommends A — meant only as a stand-in until a real phone design replaces it.

Four of the five people on the design team preferred B — one page’s design round shouldn’t change the menu for the whole app.

A — two columns (four days left, three right).

This keeps that row a medium height: on the empty week you have today, it’s 240 pixels tall; on a busy week (using invented sample data, just to test the design), about 328 pixels. The cost: because the box only splits into two columns once it’s wide enough, its shape isn’t fixed — it changes depending on how much room the page has at the time.

B — one column, all seven days in a single list.

On the empty week, this row grows to 361 pixels tall — taller than the box at the very top of the page. On a busy week the gap narrows to about 363 pixels, because a busy day’s details wrap onto a second line in a narrower column. The cost: your own plan for the page reads “tall, short, medium, short” from top to bottom; one column turns that into “tall, short, tall, short” instead.

blank week: 340·200·240·120px = 942px total, two columns 340·200·365·120px = 1067px total, one column busy week row 3: 328px two columns vs ≈363px one column

The one-column busy week is not pictured above — only its two numbers are written down, here and in the option text. It doesn’t exist as a drawing in the mockup this page is built from.

The team member who designed the grid ruled for two columns (A). The design critic pushed back — a box whose shape changes with the page’s width feels inconsistent — but the recommendation stands at two columns until you say otherwise.
