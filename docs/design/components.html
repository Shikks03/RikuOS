Two halves. Everything up to Rules is a faithful read of the source. Everything after it is the RikuOS layer — components the source doesn’t have, built from its parts, plus the decisions that produced them.

Before the tokens

What actually makes it work

Five ideas carry the whole interface. Copy these and the details follow; copy the details without these and it turns into another dark admin template.

Every number has a hue, and the hue never moves
Orange is always money leaving. Green is always money or time saved. Violet is judgement — ROI, decisions, insight. Blue is sessions and activity. Red is missing or in conflict. You learn the palette in about four seconds and then read the whole app by color alone. This is the single highest-value thing to steal.
Sections announce themselves twice
A tiny letterspaced mono label (LIVE USAGE) sits above a normal-weight sentence-case heading (Plan limits & windows). The label is the machine’s name for the section, the heading is the human one. It repeats down every page without exception, and it’s what makes a long scroll feel indexed rather than endless.
The chrome is silent so the data can shout
Sidebar, top bar, nav, labels — all near-monochrome, small, low contrast. Then a single figure jumps to 34–40px display weight in a saturated hue. There is exactly one loud thing per card and roughly three per screen.
Cards earn their borders
Only real objects get a border and a radius: a stat, a provider, a skill, an integration. Rows inside a list are separated by a hairline, not boxed. Nothing is nested more than one card deep. The result reads as a panel of instruments rather than a pile of tiles.
Decoration is data-shaped
The pretty bits are never abstract: the orange wash behind spend is a topographic reading of that spend, the green swoop is the savings curve, the constellation is the actual memory graph. Nothing is there purely to look expensive — which is precisely why it looks expensive.

Controls

Two segmented patterns doing two different jobs. The recessed one switches what you’re looking at; the floating one switches the window you’re looking through.

Ring
52px, 4px stroke, rotated -90deg, round caps, percentage centred inside in display weight. Track #1A1E25. At 0% the number goes ink 3 rather than the hue — nothing is happening, so nothing lights up.
Bar line
Window name in mono caps → used / limit with the used half in body weight → reset countdown and percentage pushed right. The 4px track sits under it. Over-limit fills to 100% and keeps the hue; it does not turn red.
Why it works
A limit has three facts — how full, how long until reset, and against what. Most dashboards show one. This shows all three without the row getting taller than 76px.

Component

Insight card

The agentic part made visible. The system proposes something, shows its reasoning, and offers two ways out. Illustration left, argument right, actions bottom-right, carousel dots for the queue.

Why we’re suggesting this

Ground
A violet gradient panel, the only place in the app where a whole section is tinted — because this is the only place the system is speaking rather than reporting.
Argument
Three bullets maximum, each a countable observation with a real number in it. No adjectives. This is what separates a suggestion from a nag.
Actions
Dismiss is a neutral outline pill; accept is outlined in green. Neither is a solid button — the system is proposing, not demanding.
Queue
Dot pagination far right; the current dot elongates into a bar. Tells you how many more are waiting without a count.

Applying it

Rules to hold, traps to avoid

Distilled from the frames. The left column is what makes it look like this; the right is what will make a rebuild look like a template with the same colors.

Hold

  • One loud figure per card, one hue per card.
  • Mono caps for anything the system names; sentence case for anything addressed to the person.
  • Address the operator directly — “your skills”, “plugged into your operator”.
  • Hairlines between rows; borders only around real objects.
  • Tabular numerals everywhere a digit repeats.
  • Say when a number is estimated, right under it.
  • Give background agents their own visual class, apart from navigation.

Avoid

  • Boxing every row — it flattens the hierarchy the hairlines create.
  • The same sparkline on every stat card; vary the graphic by the kind of number.
  • Solid accent-colored buttons. The system proposes; it doesn’t demand.
  • Turning a hue loose as decoration once it carries a meaning.
  • Left accent bars on cards — the active state here is a fill plus an inset hairline.
  • Glow on text. The glow belongs to graph nodes and status dots only.
  • A second sidebar, ever.

The colour rule
A layer takes the hue of what it is, not an arbitrary one. Freelance is orange because it’s money work. Deadlines are red because a missed one is a conflict. Classes are blue because they’re sessions. Workouts are green because they’re the healthy state. Personal is violet. The semantic palette survives intact — nothing new was invented.

Ticks, not a bar
One segment per module, 5px tall, 3px gaps. A continuous bar tells you a proportion; ticks tell you a count, which is the thing you actually want when the denominator is 9 or 12. Countable up to about 16 segments; past that, switch to a bar.
Hue from state

Two facts only: what you have (reviewers) and what’s coming (next due). A finished course says complete rather than a date, so the row doesn’t look broken.

1. The container never disappears
An empty stat card is still a card, at the same height, in the same grid position. If containers vanish when empty, the layout jumps the moment real data arrives — and the page teaches the user nothing about what will eventually live there.
2. Hue drains, structure stays
Colour in this system means “there is a signal here.” No data means no signal, so labels and figures fall to --ink-4 and hued backgrounds revert to the plain card fill. The border and radius are unchanged.
3. Em-dash, never zero
A zero is a measurement: it says you used nothing. An em-dash says nothing was measured. Confusing the two in a dashboard is a correctness bug, not a style choice.
4. One action per empty component, maximum
An outline pill, the same one used everywhere else. Never a solid call-to-action, never two competing options, never an illustration — this is a tool, and the empty state is a temporary condition rather than a landing page.
5. First run is a banner, not a state
Before anything is connected, every card would be empty, and six identical “connect something” prompts is noise. One banner at the top of the page, in the brand hue, with a single action; the cards below simply sit quiet.
Your layer

Decisions

Where the earlier open questions landed, and the one debt that was taken on deliberately. Anything marked settled is safe for Claude Code to build against without asking.

Settled

Desktop first, mobile as a later pass

RikuOS is built at desktop width against this system as documented, and gets a mobile pass afterwards. The reference stays desktop-shaped — no mobile specimens, no responsive hedging in the component code.

ComponentWhat happens at 390px
Stat heroesReflows on its own — already auto-fit / minmax. Stacks to one column, no work needed.
Skill & provider cardsSame. Reflows cleanly.

Usage rowsNeeds work. The 64px | 176px | 1fr grid has three encodings side by side. Ring and identity have to stack above the bars.
Outreach pipelineNeeds work. Four columns; the cadence strip and state tag have to move under the business name.

Six components, and five of them are the same fix: a fixed multi-column grid becoming a stack. Worth knowing the size of the debt before it comes due.

Settled

One semantic palette across all four life pages

No per-page accent hues. Orange, green, violet, blue, amber and red keep their fixed meanings everywhere, and pages are told apart by content and heading rather than colour. This is what preserves the four-second readability that makes the source work — and it is why the calendar layers could be coloured at all without inventing a second colour language.

Settled

Content and page structure live outside this document

Sections, page inventory and data shapes are already specified elsewhere for Claude Code. This reference governs how things look and behave, not what is rendered — the two should not be duplicated, and where they disagree, the content spec wins.

Open

What the three hero figures are

The source opens on money spent, money saved, net ROI — three numbers that only exist because it is an AI-cost tracker. RikuOS needs its own three: the figures worth seeing first thing in the morning, across freelance, academics and personal. Until those are named, the hero row is a shape with nothing in it.

Open

Where the agents block goes

The source rail separates background agents from navigation, which is the single best structural idea in it. RikuOS has real background work — outreach sweeps, calendar sync, Canvas pulls — but no decision yet on whether those surface as named agents in the rail, as a status row, or not at all.
