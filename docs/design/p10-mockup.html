P10 · Personal page · Mockup

The Personal page, as ruled

Eight specimens of /personal and its shell, built to the round-3 rulings so you can judge the design in a browser before any of it is built in Next.js. Every string is taken from your content document word for word; the only invented data is the week in its §13, which exists so the full page can be drawn at all. Where a sentence is proposed it is marked in the caption and it is yours to change. No JavaScript: the switches, tick boxes and forms are drawn, not wired.

Specimen 01 · State

The blank week

Your page exactly as it is today — nothing scheduled, nothing due, no to-dos yet, and one real push quoted back at you — in the default arrangement, with Personal first in the menu. Every finding here is a measured emptiness. The one hue on the page is the hero’s own: it sits at the green end of its ramp because nothing is pending (R79–R84) — a state, not an ornament, and the only other colour this page ever spends is on lateness and on a feed that did not answer.

Measured, not claimed. The four rows render at 340 · 200 · 240 · 120 pixels, so the grid is 942px tall. The second row is 200, not the 180 first planned: each of the three layer switches needs 44 pixels to be tappable, so 180 was a number the tile could not meet and 200 is its weight. Left to their own heights the tiles would measure Today 213.35, To-do 270.85, Layers 191.85, push 112.35, Next 7 days 234.25 and Done this week 51.1 — every one under its row’s weight, which is what makes the weights real: no row here is being held open by its contents.

The hero, tinted by the day — answered, and then answered again. Not one of the four alternatives specimen 01 used to carry, and no longer the two states with a line between them either: a ramp. Green at nothing pending, orange at three, red at eight, clamped there — a day with fourteen things looks like a day with eight. One and two interpolate green to orange; four through seven interpolate orange to red. The two halves are deliberately different lengths: the first three items move the colour as far as the next five do, because a day going from nothing to three things has changed more than a day going from four to eight. “Pending” is to-dos due today plus anything overdue; a scheduled event is never a task and never counts, and an overdue item counts as one like any other — it does not force the red end on its own (R79, R73).

Nine rules, frozen. The ramp ships as nine CSS classes, .pe-t0 to .pe-t8, each carrying a literal gradient pair and a literal border colour, chosen by one lookup in a frozen table. Nothing is interpolated at run time: an out-of-range count matches no rule and renders the untinted hero, which is the safe render, where an interpolation function would return an arbitrary colour and look certain about it (R80, R3’s reasoning). The three anchors are system values; the six steps between them were interpolated once, checked as the strip below, measured, and written in as hex.

For Riku · the week tile — answered: two columns (R51)

The seven days sit in two columns (four left, three right) when the tile is full width, so the third row stays “medium” as you specified. The alternative was one column at every width, which makes the third row taller than the first — your “tall, short, medium, short” would become “tall, short, tall, short”. Both are drawn above; two columns is what you chose.

On a blank week, two columns keep the third row at 240 against 361 in one column. On the busy week this ruling was judged against, the gap has since widened rather than closed: two columns now measures 240px — the disclosure collapse (R51b) leaves most days a single summary line, so the week converges on the same floor the blank week already sits on — against 362.75px in one column, where the rows were already single-line at that width and the collapse changes almost nothing. The cadence argument is stronger on the page you have today than it was when this was ruled.

The second row is 200 pixels tall, not 180 — the three layer switches need 44 pixels each to be tappable — so “short” is a little less short.
