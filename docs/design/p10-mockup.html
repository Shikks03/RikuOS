P10 · Personal page · Mockup

The Personal page, as ruled

Eight specimens of /personal and its shell, built to the round-3 rulings so you can judge the design in a browser before any of it is built in Next.js. Every string is taken from your content document word for word; the only invented data is the week in its §13, which exists so the full page can be drawn at all. Where a sentence is proposed it is marked in the caption and it is yours to change. No JavaScript: the switches, tick boxes and forms are drawn, not wired.

Specimen 01 · State

The blank week

Your page exactly as it is today — nothing scheduled, nothing due, no to-dos yet, and one real push quoted back at you — in the default arrangement, with Personal first in the menu. There is not one coloured thing on the page: every finding here is a measured emptiness, and colour on this page is reserved for lateness and for a feed that did not answer.

Measured, not claimed. The four rows render at 340 · 200 · 240 · 120 pixels, so the grid is 942px tall. The second row is 200, not the 180 first planned: each of the three layer switches needs 44 pixels to be tappable, so 180 was a number the tile could not meet and 200 is its weight. For Riku · the week tile

Ruled: the seven days sit in two columns (four left, three right) when the tile is full width, so the third row stays “medium” as you specified. The alternative is one column at every width, which makes the third row taller than the first — your “tall, short, medium, short” would become “tall, short, tall, short”. Both are drawn above.

On a blank week, two columns keep the third row at 240 against 361 in one column; on a busy week the difference shrinks to 328 against 363, because every full day wraps to two lines in a narrower column. The cadence argument is strongest on the page you have today.

For Riku · the phone

The menu on the left is a fixed strip 170 pixels wide on every screen, phone included. On a phone that leaves about a third of the screen for the page.

Option A — fix the menu now: on a narrow screen the menu becomes a thin bar across the top and the page gets the whole width; the morning works — push buzzes, open the page, read the day, tick with one thumb; the cost is that the coloured worker badges have nowhere to sit on a phone and are hidden there until the proper phone design.

Option B — leave it: Personal is a laptop page for now; on a phone you read the notification itself; the phone layout gets its own design round later; the cost is that the reason this page exists — phone buzzes at 07:00, this is where you go next — does not work yet.

Both are drawn above. The lead’s recommendation is A, as a stopgap the phone pass replaces; four of five of the team preferred B, on the grounds that a page phase should not change the shell.

With the menu strip beside the page, the page is too narrow to open the add-event or add-to-do form at all — both buttons are switched off there; with the thin top bar, both forms open.
